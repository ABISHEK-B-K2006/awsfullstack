⚠️ Problem Statement

Critical Gaps in Legacy Insurance Systems

Traditional insurer architectures suffer from financial exposure, slow turnaround, and lack of transparency.

1. Concurrent Payout Race Conditions
When multiple claims are approved simultaneously against a single policy, legacy databases experience phantom reads and over-disbursements exceeding policy caps.
2. Fragmented Stakeholder Silos
Policyholders, underwriters, field adjusters, and claims directors operate on disconnected tools, causing 30+ day claim settlement delays.
3. Static Risk Assessment
Manual actuarial rate calculation without real-time risk factor calibration (age, occupational hazards, prior claims history).
4. Lack of Public Transparency
Policyholders lack real-time visibility into remaining capacity buffers and live investigation stages.
🗣️ Speaker Note (45s): "In real-world insurance firms, the biggest threat is financial overdraw—two claims approved at the same second exceeding a ₹2,50,000 policy buffer. Furthermore, policyholders wait weeks without knowing claim status, and risk scoring is done manually on spreadsheets."

⚡ Core Innovation

Transactional Concurrency & Zero-Overdraw

How InsureFlow mathematically prevents financial leakage during peak adjudications.

Pessimistic Write Lock Engine

During claim settlement, the backend invokes a row-level database lock:

@Lock(LockModeType.PESSIMISTIC_WRITE)
@Query("SELECT p FROM InsurancePolicy p WHERE p.id = :id")
Optional<InsurancePolicy> findByIdWithPessimisticWrite(@Param("id") Long id);
Atomic Buffer Deduction Lifecycle
  • Step 1: Acquire row lock on target policy.
  • Step 2: Verify requested payout ≤ live remaining buffer.
  • Step 3: Atomically deduct remaining capacity and commit.
  • Step 4: Automatically schedule electronic wire disbursement.
🗣️ Speaker Note (45s): "This is our core technical innovation. Rather than relying on optimistic version checks that cause rejected retries, we use Pessimistic Write Locks directly on the policy table. When Manager approves a payout, the remaining buffer is locked, verified, and deducted atomically. Zero overdraw is mathematically guaranteed."
